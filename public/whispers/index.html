Fifty voices can still be one source

October 3, 2026 · Taylor Burton · TSCHECH Labs

A stock story can get exciting before it gets clear. Somebody mentions a possible customer. Other people repeat it. Soon the claim seems familiar enough that nobody remembers where it started.

I want to follow that process more carefully. Today I rebuilt the plan for Whisper Radar with feedback from Claude and Sol. The project will keep a public ledger of stock claims and test whether growing discussion across different communities can give us an earlier indication of broader attention.

The ledger matters because popularity and support are different measurements. If fifty accounts repeat the same original post, we have observed fifty accounts discussing it. We have not found fifty independent confirmations.

Fictional example. Every number below is invented.

EXMP is an invented ticker. Discussion grows from 12 to 46 accounts, reaching five communities instead of two. The claim is that a major customer has signed a contract. Most of the discussion refers back to one source, and a recorded search finds no supporting primary document.

The honest reading would be that observed attention grew and the claimed contract remains unverified. It would not tell us that the claim is false. It would not tell us whether the stock is cheap, expensive, or about to move. If supporting evidence appears later, the ledger changes with a dated update.

Claude’s most useful suggestion was to make that ledger the publication itself. The model can help choose what to investigate, but readers should be able to inspect the claim and evidence without trusting a mysterious score. We are also keeping an outcomes archive, including misses and cases where missing data prevents a conclusion.

Sol’s rebuild makes the first experiment smaller. We will compare a four-component attention model against simple post and account counters. More complicated features have to earn their place. If a mention counter works just as well, that is the model we should use.

Today’s work is a design, not a stock discovery. There is no live feed, backtest, or validated predictor yet. Research on social diffusion gives us reasons to test the idea, but no assurance that our version will find an advantage. [1] [2] The immediate job is to verify the data we can consistently observe.

The next work session is on my calendar for Tuesday, October 6. This opening post introduces the question; next we will document what the system can and cannot see. Once we have real observations, we can publish findings with their dates, coverage, and comparison baselines.

Follow along if you like experiments with a visible record. We will trace a story, check its support, and return to it after the excitement has had time to settle. The wrong calls will stay on the page.

AI assistance: Sol and Claude helped with project design and drafting. The linked research was reviewed by AI at the abstract level. The EXMP scenario is fictional, and this post reports no measured stock findings. Whisper Radar studies attention and evidence; it does not provide investment recommendations.

Research background

  1. Varol, Ferrara, Menczer, and Flammini. Early Detection of Promoted Campaigns on Social Media (2017). Background on diffusion features; it does not validate this stock model.
  2. Gianstefani, Longo, and Riccaboni. The echo chamber effect resounds on financial markets: a social media alert system for meme stocks (2022). Relevant equity case studies, with limited generalizability.
